Food Intake — Privacy Policy

Last updated 6 October 2026

Food Intake is a personal nutrition log. This policy says exactly what happens to your information. It is short because the app collects very little, and nothing at all for advertising or analytics.

What never leaves your device

Apple Health data

If you grant access, the app reads your steps, Activity rings and sleep from Apple Health purely to display them beside your food log.

This data is read-only and never transmitted. The app never writes to Apple Health, never stores its own copy, never puts it in iCloud, and never sends it to any server — including for photo analysis. It is read when you open a day and shown on screen.

Because of this, a day exported from the Mac app contains no health information: only the device that read it can export it. Revoking access in iOS Settings simply stops it appearing.

What is stored in your own iCloud

Your food log — meals, foods, nutrition figures, portions, notes and the photos you take — is kept on your device and synced through your own private iCloud account (Apple's CloudKit private database), so it reaches your other devices.

This is your iCloud, not ours. The developer has no access to it and cannot read it. Apple's handling of that data is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

What leaves your device, and why

Food photos, for nutrition analysis

To estimate what you ate, the app sends the photo — and any note you typed with it — to Anthropic's Claude API, which returns the estimate. There are two routes, depending on how you set the app up:

  • With your own Anthropic API key: the photo goes directly from your device to Anthropic. Nothing passes through the developer's systems.
  • Signed in with Apple: the photo passes through a small proxy service run by the developer, which forwards it to Anthropic so you do not need your own key.

The proxy does not store, log or retain your photos or notes. It passes them straight through and keeps only the account and usage records described below.

Anthropic processes the photo in order to produce the estimate. See Anthropic's Privacy Policy.

Sign in with Apple (only if you use the shared service)

If you sign in rather than supplying your own API key, the proxy stores:

  • the Apple user identifier Apple issues for you — an opaque ID specific to this app;
  • the name you chose to share at sign-in;
  • the email address Apple provides, which is the relay address (@privaterelay.appleid.com) if you chose to hide your real one;
  • a hashed session token, so the app can stay signed in — the token itself is not stored; and
  • counts and costs of analyses you have run.

The counts exist only to stop the shared service being abused and to keep it inside a monthly budget. None of this is created if you use your own API key, and none of it is used for marketing or shared with anyone.

Operational logs

The proxy writes technical logs — timestamps, error messages, which model answered — kept for 30 days and then deleted automatically. These logs do not contain your photos, your notes or your food data.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting.
  • No advertising, ad identifiers, or data used for advertising.
  • No tracking of you across other apps or websites.
  • No selling or renting of your information to anyone, ever.
  • No health data on any server, in any form.

Deleting your information

  • Your food log and photos: delete individual entries in the app, or delete the app and remove its iCloud data in Settings → Apple Account → iCloud → Manage Storage.
  • Apple Health access: revoke it in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Food Intake. Nothing was stored, so nothing needs deleting.
  • Your shared-service account: email the address below and the account, its name, email and usage records will be deleted. Revoking the app in Settings → Apple Account → Sign in with Apple also stops it being used.

Children

Food Intake is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes affecting information already collected will be announced in the app.
